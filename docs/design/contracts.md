# Contracts

## Purpose

Several Solidity contracts give the system its on-chain ground truth. `KRWTestStablecoin` (tKRW) is the settlement asset: an ERC-20 test stablecoin whose issuance is controlled by a single issuer, whose transfers can be authorized by signature, and which carries issuer freeze and an opt-in transfer allowlist for regulatory control. `IdentityRegistry` records which addresses are registered agents, so a gateway can refuse payments from unknown payers. Three more contracts support asset delivery and on-chain enforcement: `RWATestAsset` (tRWA) is the asset a payer receives; `EligibilityRegistry` gates who may receive it, with agents able to inherit eligibility from a delegator; `DvPSettlement` settles payment and delivery atomically; and `DelegatedSpend` enforces a spending mandate on chain. Without them there is nothing to settle against, no on-chain notion of who is paying, and nothing to deliver.

## Behavior

tKRW is an ERC-20 with zero decimals whose `mint` and `burn` are restricted to the issuer, with a `Pausable` switch and a two-step issuer handover (`transferIssuer` then `acceptIssuer`). Its distinguishing feature is EIP-3009 `transferWithAuthorization`: the token moves when anyone submits a transfer signed off-chain by the payer, and the contract records each authorization nonce so a signature settles at most once (`AuthorizationUsed`).

tKRW also carries regulatory controls: the issuer can freeze an account (`setFrozen`), which bars it from sending or receiving on the transfer paths while leaving issuer mint and burn available so a frozen balance can still be seized or redeemed, and can enable an opt-in allowlist (`setAllowlistEnabled`, `setAllowed`) that, while on, admits a transfer only when both parties are listed (a mint checks its recipient).

`IdentityRegistry` maps an address to a registration flag and an agent-card URL. `register(agentCardURL)` registers `msg.sender` and calling it again updates the URL; `isRegistered` and `agentCard` are the read surface, and `AgentRegistered` is emitted on every registration.

`RWATestAsset` (tRWA) reuses the minimal tKRW skeleton, an ERC-20 with issuer mint and burn, so the same ledger tooling reconciles it. It holds an immutable eligibility registry address; when set, `_transfer` and `mint` require the recipient to be eligible, in the spirit of ERC-3643's recipient-centric check. `EligibilityRegistry` stores `eligible[addr]` set by a registrar and `delegatedBy[agent]` set by any account that sponsors an agent, and computes `isEligible(a)` as `eligible[a] || eligible[delegatedBy[a]]`, so inheritance is dynamic: turning off a delegator's eligibility removes the agent's at once, with no revocation to propagate.

`DvPSettlement` settles payment and delivery in one transaction: `settleAndDeliver` runs the tKRW `transferWithAuthorization` (payment, with the seller as recipient) then the tRWA `transferFrom` (delivery, from the seller who approved this contract), reverting the whole call if either fails. `DelegatedSpend` holds a delegator's tKRW deposit and a mandate (agent, per-payment cap, validity window, payee allowlist, cumulative budget); its `pay` runs the same checks the gateway's mandate policy runs, in the same cheapest-first order, and moves the deposit to the payee on success.

## Design decisions

**EIP-3009 rather than allowance-based transfers.** Requiring a paying agent to hold gas is unrealistic, so the payer and the settlement executor are separated: the payer only signs, and whoever submits the settlement pays for gas. This matches how the exact scheme of x402 uses USDC's EIP-3009, and tKRW implements the same interface directly rather than imitating it behind a custom API. Random 32-byte nonces let several authorizations be prepared in parallel without collisions, which sequential nonces would serialize. The interception window of `transferWithAuthorization` (anyone who sees a signed authorization may submit it) is a known limit; closing it with `receiveWithAuthorization` is tracked in ROADMAP.md.

**A registry that mirrors the deployed ones.** The registry is intentionally minimal, in the spirit of ERC-8004: a flag and an opaque card URL. Agents self-register with `msg.sender` because that is the registration model of the deployed public registries this contract stands in for; a sponsored-registration path would have created a local-only interface and broken the later swap to a real registry. The card URL is stored without being fetched or validated, keeping the contract free of any oracle-like duty.

**Issuer controls stay simple and explicit.** Mint and burn are issuer-only and pausable, and the issuer handover takes two steps so a typo in the new address cannot brick issuance. Freeze and the opt-in allowlist reproduce the shape of a regulated stablecoin's controls without its full compliance surface: freeze exempts issuer mint and burn so a frozen balance stays seizable, and the allowlist is off by default so ordinary runs are unchanged. Reserve attestation remains out of scope and is listed in the README's Scope and limitations.

**On-chain enforcement mirrors the off-chain policy, with two named differences.** `DelegatedSpend` shares the mandate vocabulary and check order with the gateway so the two can be compared directly, and a parity test asserts they agree on a shared case table. Two differences are intentional: resource scoping stays off chain, since the chain cannot see URLs, and the cumulative budget uses a fixed window rather than the gateway's sliding one, to avoid the gas cost of tracking per-payment timestamps. The DvP contract works on a seller approval rather than binding the payment authorization to itself; that interception window is the same one `transferWithAuthorization` has, and closing it with a receive-style path is tracked in ROADMAP.md.

**Artifacts are compiled once and embedded.** `contracts/compile.js` compiles the contracts with solc-js, pinned to `evmVersion: 'paris'` because the go-ethereum simulated backend does not support PUSH0, and writes each contract's ABI and bytecode into its Go package (`token/`, `registry/`, `asset/`, `eligibility/`, `dvp/`, `spend/`). The Go bindings use `bind.BoundContract` directly instead of abigen output, which keeps the call sites small and reviewable, and the EIP-712 domain separator is always read from the deployed contract rather than reconstructed, so bindings cannot drift from what the chain will verify.

## Limits

The contracts verify flows; they are unaudited and not production code. tKRW's zero decimals keep demo arithmetic readable at the cost of realism. The registry carries no reputation or richer identity surface, and a deployed ERC-8004 registry replaces it behind the gateway's read-only reader interface rather than by matching its full ABI.
